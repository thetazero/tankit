# Design direction

**Rugged, solid metal, clear exposed machinery.**

Inspired by the look of the tech in Villeneuve's *Dune* films (the
ornithopters are the touchstone): the feel of the machines, not the setting,
story or any specific designs.

## Principles

- **Silhouette first.** A machine should be recognizable as a speck in the
  distance, or from its shadow alone. Get there through proportion contrast: a
  dense, compact core with a few long, straight elements (barrels, blades,
  legs). Keep it low and long, with strong horizontal lines.
- **Built from panels.** The shape itself is made of panels: a faceted
  carapace of separate thick plates, each at its own angle, with dark gaps
  between them and edges lapping over their neighbors. Not a box with plates
  stuck on, and not a stack of shrinking plates. Vary panel sizes: long
  plates on long runs, smaller ones where the form turns (prow, shoulders).
  Never a uniform grid.
- **Detail in three scales.** Big: the silhouette. Medium: layered plates,
  hatches, bins, louvers, rails, spread over the whole body. Small: tight
  clusters (cupola periscopes, latches, hooks, bolts) at a few focal points.
- **Machinery is honest.** Where things move, show how:
  pivots, arms, actuators, springs, gearing. You should be able to see how it
  works, and it should actually move when the machine does (suspension
  working, rams extending, recoil sliding). It supports the body; it is not
  the only interesting thing on it.
- **Creature logic, industrial parts.** A familiar body plan (legs, a head, a
  posture) made entirely of hard machine parts. A row of suspension arms raked
  the same way should read like legs.
- **Tone, not color.** The machines are near-monochrome, in the same muted,
  dusty family as the world. Form comes from light and shadow: hard sun, crisp
  cast shadows, lit top planes over a dark, dense underside. Team identity is
  a faint cast at most.
- **Subtle, even wear.** A fine film of dust and sun-bleaching over
  everything, a little heavier on top and low down. No cartoon chips, scratches
  or high-contrast grime.
- **Weight and scale.** Things sit low and heavy. Thick plates, nothing
  hollow, plastic or shiny. Only machined parts (rods, pins) catch a little
  light.

**Bright color and glow are reserved for combat** (shots, hits, shields,
damage), so the action stays readable against the muted machines.
